Question A1

What is the CiteScore of the journal where your selected research paper was published/to be published, and what does it indicate about the journal's impact? If a journal published 500 documents in the last 4 years and received 5,000 citations.

Solution:

CiteScore is a metric that measures the average citations received per document published in a journal.

The formula to calculate CiteScore is:

CiteScore = Total citations in the last 4 years / Total documents published in the last 4 years

Given:

  • Total citations = 5,000
  • Total documents = 500

Calculation:

CiteScore = 5,000 / 500 = 10.0

Interpretation:

A CiteScore of 10.0 indicates that on average, each document published in this journal received 10 citations in the last 4 years. This suggests:

  • The journal has a significant impact in its field
  • Articles published in this journal are frequently cited by other researchers
  • The journal is likely to be considered prestigious within its discipline

Generally, CiteScore values can be interpreted as:

  • 0-1: Low impact
  • 1-3: Moderate impact
  • 3-6: Good impact
  • 6-10: Very good impact
  • 10+: Excellent impact

Question A3

Calculate and WAP for Spearman's Rank Correlation (ρ) of the following data.

Note: The actual data was not provided in the exam paper. For demonstration purposes, I'll use sample data:

Student Math Rank Physics Rank
A 1 2
B 2 1
C 3 4
D 4 3
E 5 6
F 6 5

Solution:

Spearman's rank correlation coefficient (ρ) measures the strength and direction of association between two ranked variables.

The formula is:

ρ = 1 - [6 × Σd² / (n(n² - 1))]

where:
d = difference between the two ranks for each observation
n = number of observations

Step-by-Step Calculation:
  1. Calculate the difference (d) between ranks for each pair
  2. Square each difference (d²)
  3. Sum all squared differences (Σd²)
  4. Apply the formula
Student Math Rank (X) Physics Rank (Y) d (X-Y) d²
A 1 2 -1 1
B 2 1 1 1
C 3 4 -1 1
D 4 3 1 1
E 5 6 -1 1
F 6 5 1 1
Total (Σd²) 6

Now apply the formula with n = 6:

ρ = 1 - [6 × 6 / (6 × (36 - 1))] = 1 - (36 / 210) = 1 - 0.1714 = 0.8286

Interpretation:

The Spearman's rank correlation coefficient is 0.829, indicating a strong positive correlation between math and physics rankings. Students who rank high in math tend to also rank high in physics, and vice versa.

WAP (Write a Program) in Python:

import scipy.stats as stats

# Sample data
math_ranks = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]
physics_ranks = [2, 1, 4, 3, 6, 5]

# Calculate Spearman's rho
rho, p_value = stats.spearmanr(math_ranks, physics_ranks)

print(f"Spearman's rho: {rho:.4f}")
print(f"P-value: {p_value:.4f}")

Output would be:

Spearman's rho: 0.8286
P-value: 0.0416

Question A4

Calculate and WAP for Chi-square goodness-of-fit test (χ²) of the following data. The outcomes of 60 die rolls are as follows:

Note: The actual die roll outcomes were not specified in the exam paper. For demonstration, I'll assume the following distribution:

Die Face Observed Frequency
1 8
2 12
3 9
4 11
5 10
6 10
Total 60

Solution:

The Chi-square goodness-of-fit test determines whether observed frequencies differ significantly from expected frequencies.

For a fair die, we expect each face to appear equally often:

Expected frequency for each face = Total rolls / Number of faces = 60 / 6 = 10

The Chi-square statistic is calculated as:

χ² = Σ[(Observed - Expected)² / Expected]
Step-by-Step Calculation:
Die Face Observed (O) Expected (E) O - E (O - E)² (O - E)²/E
1 8 10 -2 4 0.4
2 12 10 2 4 0.4
3 9 10 -1 1 0.1
4 11 10 1 1 0.1
5 10 10 0 0 0.0
6 10 10 0 0 0.0
Total χ² 1.0

Degrees of freedom (df) = Number of categories - 1 = 6 - 1 = 5

Using a Chi-square distribution table or calculator, we find the critical value at α = 0.05 with df = 5 is 11.07.

Conclusion:

Since our calculated χ² (1.0) is less than the critical value (11.07), we fail to reject the null hypothesis. There is no significant evidence to suggest that the die is unfair.

WAP (Write a Program) in Python:

import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import chisquare

# Observed frequencies
observed = np.array([8, 12, 9, 11, 10, 10])

# Expected frequencies (equal probability for fair die)
expected = np.array([10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10])

# Perform Chi-square test
chi2_stat, p_value = chisquare(observed, f_exp=expected)

print(f"Chi-square statistic: {chi2_stat:.4f}")
print(f"P-value: {p_value:.4f}")

# Compare to critical value (alpha=0.05, df=5)
critical_value = 11.07
print(f"Critical value (α=0.05, df=5): {critical_value}")

if chi2_stat > critical_value:
    print("Reject null hypothesis - die may be unfair")
else:
    print("Fail to reject null hypothesis - no evidence die is unfair")

Output would be:

Chi-square statistic: 1.0000
P-value: 0.9626
Critical value (α=0.05, df=5): 11.07
Fail to reject null hypothesis - no evidence die is unfair

Question C1

What is the SNIP of the journal you chose, and why is this metric useful when comparing journals across different fields? A journal published 120 articles in the last 4 years. These articles received a total of 1,560 citations during the same period. What is the cite score?

Solution:

SNIP (Source Normalized Impact per Paper) is a journal metric that measures contextual citation impact by weighting citations based on the total number of citations in a field.

Why SNIP is useful for cross-field comparisons:

  • Normalizes for differences in citation practices between fields (some fields cite more than others)
  • Accounts for the frequency of citations in the journal's subject area
  • Helps compare journals across disciplines more fairly than raw citation counts
  • Considers the database coverage in the journal's field

CiteScore Calculation:

Given:

  • Total citations in last 4 years = 1,560
  • Total documents published in last 4 years = 120

Formula:

CiteScore = Total citations / Total documents = 1,560 / 120 = 13.0

Interpretation:

The journal has a CiteScore of 13.0, indicating high impact as each article receives an average of 13 citations within 4 years of publication.

Question C3

What is the similarity index? Explain plagiarism tools.

Solution:

Similarity Index:

The similarity index is a percentage score that indicates how much of a submitted document matches existing sources in a database. It's generated by plagiarism detection software to help identify potential plagiarism.

Key aspects of similarity index:

  • Represents the percentage of text that matches other sources
  • Doesn't necessarily indicate plagiarism - proper citations will also be flagged
  • Helps identify areas that need proper attribution
  • Thresholds vary by institution (commonly 10-20% is acceptable)

Plagiarism Detection Tools:

Tool Description Features
Turnitin Widely used in academic institutions Compares against internet, academic papers, student submissions; provides similarity report
iThenticate Professional plagiarism checker Used by publishers and researchers; checks against scholarly content
Grammarly Writing assistant with plagiarism check Checks against web pages; provides writing suggestions
Copyscape Web content plagiarism checker Specialized for online content; checks for duplicate web content
PlagScan Comprehensive plagiarism detection Used by educational institutions; detailed reports

How Plagiarism Tools Work:

  1. Text Analysis: The submitted document is broken down into smaller chunks (sentences or phrases)
  2. Database Comparison: Each chunk is compared against:
    • Internet sources
    • Academic databases
    • Previously submitted works
    • Published books and journals
  3. Matching Algorithm: Identifies matching or similar text segments
  4. Report Generation: Creates a report highlighting matched text and sources
  5. Similarity Score Calculation: Computes the percentage of matched text

Limitations:

  • Cannot detect properly cited content as plagiarism
  • May miss paraphrased content if wording is significantly changed
  • Database coverage varies by tool
  • Cannot assess intent (plagiarism vs. proper citation)

Question C4

Calculate and WAP for Paired Student's t-test of the following data.

Before = [70, 68, 80, 72, 65, 74]

After = [75, 74, 85, 78, 70, 79]

Solution:

The paired t-test compares the means of two related groups to determine if there is a statistically significant difference.

Step-by-Step Calculation:

  1. Calculate the differences (After - Before)
  2. Compute the mean and standard deviation of the differences
  3. Calculate the t-statistic
  4. Compare to critical t-value or calculate p-value
Subject Before After Difference (d)
1 70 75 5
2 68 74 6
3 80 85 5
4 72 78 6
5 65 70 5
6 74 79 5

Calculate mean difference (d̄) and standard deviation (s_d):

d̄ = (5 + 6 + 5 + 6 + 5 + 5) / 6 = 32 / 6 = 5.333
s_d = √[Σ(d_i - d̄)² / (n-1)] = √[(0.111 + 0.444 + 0.111 + 0.444 + 0.111 + 0.111)/5] = √[1.333/5] = √0.2666 = 0.516

Calculate t-statistic:

t = d̄ / (s_d/√n) = 5.333 / (0.516/√6) = 5.333 / 0.2106 = 25.32

Degrees of freedom = n - 1 = 5

Critical t-value (two-tailed, α=0.05, df=5) = 2.571

Conclusion:

Since our calculated t-value (25.32) is much greater than the critical value (2.571), we reject the null hypothesis. There is a statistically significant difference between the before and after measurements.

WAP (Write a Program) in Python:

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

# Data
before = np.array([70, 68, 80, 72, 65, 74])
after = np.array([75, 74, 85, 78, 70, 79])

# Perform paired t-test
t_stat, p_value = stats.ttest_rel(after, before)

print(f"t-statistic: {t_stat:.4f}")
print(f"p-value: {p_value:.6f}")

# Compare to alpha = 0.05
alpha = 0.05
if p_value < alpha:
    print("Reject null hypothesis - significant difference exists")
else:
    print("Fail to reject null hypothesis - no significant difference")

Output would be:

t-statistic: 25.3165
p-value: 0.000001
Reject null hypothesis - significant difference exists

Question D3

Calculate and WAP for Pearson correlation coefficient (r) of the following data.

Student Hours Studied (X) Test Score (Y)
A 2 65
B 3 70
C 5 75
D 6 78
E 8 85

Solution:

Pearson correlation coefficient (r) measures the linear correlation between two variables, ranging from -1 to 1.

The formula is:

r = [nΣXY - (ΣX)(ΣY)] / √[nΣX² - (ΣX)²][nΣY² - (ΣY)²]
Step-by-Step Calculation:
  1. Calculate necessary sums
  2. Apply the formula
  3. Interpret the result
Student X Y X² Y² XY
A 2 65 4 4225 130
B 3 70 9 4900 210
C 5 75 25 5625 375
D 6 78 36 6084 468
E 8 85 64 7225 680
Total 24 373 138 28,059 1,863

Now apply the formula with n = 5:

r = [5×1863 - 24×373] / √[5×138 - 24²][5×28059 - 373²]
= [9315 - 8952] / √[690 - 576][140295 - 139129]
= 363 / √[114][1166]
= 363 / √132,924
= 363 / 364.587
= 0.996

Interpretation:

The Pearson correlation coefficient is 0.996, indicating an extremely strong positive correlation between hours studied and test scores. As study hours increase, test scores tend to increase as well.

WAP (Write a Program) in Python:

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

# Data
hours = np.array([2, 3, 5, 6, 8])
scores = np.array([65, 70, 75, 78, 85])

# Calculate Pearson r
r, p_value = stats.pearsonr(hours, scores)

print(f"Pearson correlation coefficient (r): {r:.4f}")
print(f"P-value: {p_value:.6f}")

# Interpretation
if abs(r) >= 0.8:
    strength = "very strong"
elif abs(r) >= 0.6:
    strength = "strong"
elif abs(r) >= 0.4:
    strength = "moderate"
elif abs(r) >= 0.2:
    strength = "weak"
else:
    strength = "very weak or none"

direction = "positive" if r > 0 else "negative"

print(f"Interpretation: {strength} {direction} correlation")

Output would be:

Pearson correlation coefficient (r): 0.9959
P-value: 0.000082
Interpretation: very strong positive correlation

Question D4

Calculate and WAP for Coefficient of Determination (R²) of the following data.

Student Hours Studied (X) Test Score (Y)
A 2 65
B 3 70
C 5 75
D 6 78
E 8 85

Solution:

The coefficient of determination (R²) is the square of the Pearson correlation coefficient (r). It represents the proportion of variance in the dependent variable that's predictable from the independent variable.

From Question D3, we calculated r = 0.996

Thus:

R² = r² = (0.996)² = 0.992

Interpretation:

An R² of 0.992 means that 99.2% of the variation in test scores can be explained by the number of hours studied. This indicates an extremely strong relationship between the variables.

WAP (Write a Program) in Python:

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

# Data
hours = np.array([2, 3, 5, 6, 8])
scores = np.array([65, 70, 75, 78, 85])

# Calculate Pearson r
r, _ = stats.pearsonr(hours, scores)

# Calculate R-squared
r_squared = r**2

print(f"Coefficient of determination (R²): {r_squared:.4f}")
print(f"{r_squared*100:.1f}% of the variance in test scores is explained by study hours")

Output would be:

Coefficient of determination (R²): 0.9919
99.2% of the variance in test scores is explained by study hours
